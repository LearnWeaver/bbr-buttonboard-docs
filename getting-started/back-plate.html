Back plate for the leads#

Leads that drop through the openGrid board hang loose behind the panel. The back plate holds them flat: a plate the size of your board, standing a few millimetres off its back, held by snap-in standoffs.

  • The plate has a hole on every cell, so a standoff can go in any free cell, around the cells your drop tiles already use.
  • Each standoff snaps into the back of the board like a low cable tile, and its post clips up through the plate’s hole.
  • The post is hollow, 10.4 mm across. A lead can still come through the board at a standoff’s cell, so placing a standoff never costs you a drop position.

You need a full openGrid board. The standoffs snap into the back of the board, as the low cable tiles do. A lite board is single-sided and has nothing to snap into.

Make your plate#

Set the size of your board in cells, then download the two parts. The page builds the plate for that size. You print one plate and several standoffs from the standoff STL.

The generator needs JavaScript. Without it, the 5 × 5 plate and its standoffs are in case-back-plate.zip.

  • Gap is the space between the back of the board and the plate. At the default 5 mm, twisted leads, a plug lying flat, and the 3.75 mm low cable tiles all fit under it. A bigger gap leaves more room, and the clip gets easier to push in.
  • Plate hole is for your printer. Make it a little bigger if the clips won’t go through, or a little smaller if the plate rattles.

Printing#

  • Plate: flat on the bed.
  • Standoffs: the way they sit, with the snap on the bed. They print without support material.
  • The clip has to flex as it goes through the plate. At the 5 mm gap that is near the limit of PLA. If PLA clips crack, print the standoffs in PETG.

Fitting it#

  1. Snap the standoffs into the back of the board: one near each corner, and more across the middle on a big board. The generator suggests how many.
  2. Route your leads across the back. A lead can come through any free cell, or up through a standoff’s post.
  3. Lay the plate over the posts and press down over each one until it clicks.

To take the plate off, squeeze the clip at the top of each post and lift that corner.
