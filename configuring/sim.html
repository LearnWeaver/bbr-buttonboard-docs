Flight and driving sims#

In sim mode the board is a large joystick: 128 buttons, 8 axes and a hat switch. That is the most a single joystick can report on Windows, and every flight and driving sim reads it. Use it for a button box, a cockpit panel, or a set of switches.

To turn it on, see USB modes: pick Sim (128 buttons), Save, unplug and plug back in.

Sim mode has not yet been tested in a game. Check it in your sim’s controls screen before relying on it.

Button numbers#

Sims bind every control themselves, in the game’s own settings. So in sim mode you do not choose button numbers. Every key is its own button, numbered automatically. Select a key in Configure to see its number, then bind it in the game.

The numbers run tile by tile, in address order:

  • each tile’s keys, in key order;
  • then two buttons for each encoder: one for clockwise, one for anticlockwise.

For example, an mx9 at address 1, an arc4 at 2 and an enc1 at 3 give buttons 1–9, 10–13, 14 (the knob’s push) and 15 and 16 (its turns).

Unplugging a tile does not move anyone’s numbers. The board remembers the tile and keeps its place, so your game’s bindings survive. Add new tiles at the end of the chain. To swap a failed tile, use replace (see Swapping and replacing tiles) so the new one takes the old one’s address.

Keys past button 128 send nothing.

The 32 button numbers, groups and combos from the other modes do not apply in sim. LED colours, display tiles and debounce do.

Toggles#

Tick Toggle in the key’s inspector and the key latches: each press switches the button on or off, and letting go does nothing. Use it for landing gear, lights or a master arm on an ordinary key. Toggles are all off when the board starts and after any change to the configuration. Up to 32 keys can be toggles.

The hat switch#

Four keys can make a hat switch, which flight sims use to look around. Select a key and choose Up, Right, Down or Left under Hat.

  • One key pressed points the hat that way. Two neighbouring keys pressed together give a diagonal.
  • Opposite keys cancel each other out.
  • A key on the hat stops being a button.

The hat can be built one key at a time.

Knobs and axes#

Each click of an encoder is a short press of one of its two buttons, clockwise or anticlockwise. Games bind a rotary control, such as radio tuning or trim, as two buttons.

To use a knob as an axis instead, bind it to an axis in its inspector. In sim there are eight axes: X, Y, Z, Rx, Ry, Rz, Slider and Dial. A knob on an axis keeps its two button numbers, but never presses them. Thumbsticks on a stick1 tile can use all eight axes too.

frc reports only the first four axes, so a knob on axis 5 to 8 moves only in sim.
