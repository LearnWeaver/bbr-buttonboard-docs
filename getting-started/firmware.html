Updating the firmware#

Two different things get updated, in two different ways. Both live on the Firmware screen of the config app.

Tiles#

Tiles update over the bus — the same cable they are already plugged into. Nothing is unplugged, no programmer is needed, and one tile updates at a time while the rest of the chain carries on.

  1. Open Firmware in the config app.
  2. Any tile running older firmware than the images the site carries is listed with an Update button.
  3. Press it and wait. Progress is shown as it goes.

The tile drops out of the chain for a few seconds and rejoins when it is done. Its address and its bindings are untouched.

A tile that loses power partway through an update is not bricked. The updater writes to a staging area and only commits after the whole image has arrived and its checksum matches; a tile that never gets that far comes back on its old firmware.

The main module#

The main module updates over USB, using the same drag-and-drop mechanism as any RP2040 board.

  1. Press Update main module in the config app. The board reboots into its bootloader, and the COM port disappears — this is expected, and the config app will tell you it has lost the connection.
  2. A USB drive called RPI-RP2 appears on your computer.
  3. Drag the .uf2 file onto it. The drive disappears and the board restarts on the new firmware.

Your configuration — bindings, LED colours, USB personality — is stored separately from the firmware and survives the update.

If the config app cannot reach the board at all, you can get to the same place by hand: unplug the board, hold the BOOTSEL button, plug it back in, and release. RPI-RP2 appears the same way.

Which versions am I on?#

The Connect screen reports the main module’s firmware version, and Diagnose reports each tile’s. Mixed versions across tiles are fine — the board checks that every tile speaks a compatible protocol and flags any that does not, rather than assuming.
